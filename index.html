    CrossfireLab

    Three fighters learn a top-down firefight from nothing. They see only through rays, choose their own loadouts, and keep what they learn between visits.

    How the training works

    Each fighter casts 14 rays. Every ray reports distance to a wall, whether it touches an enemy, and whether it crosses fire or a live grenade. A small neural network turns those readings into movement, turning, firing, weapon swaps and utility throws.

    Each fighter also has a target sensor: a line-of-sight ray to the nearest enemy in its 120 degree view, giving the angle off its crosshair, the distance and the enemy's health.

    Training uses evolution strategies. Every fighter has one main brain. It makes 16 slightly different copies, 8 pushed one way and 8 the opposite way, and lives out one copy per life. Once every copy has played, the main brain moves a small step toward the copies that scored best. Small steps averaged over 16 lives keep a lucky life from throwing the brain off course.

    Every life is scored from specific events, listed below. Results (the first group) always count in full. Coaching points (the second group) teach skills like finding enemies and aiming. They start at full strength and fade to 35% over each fighter's first 300 brain updates, so once the basics are learned, winning fights matters most.

    Accuracy is hits divided by shots, with each shotgun pellet counted as a shot. Each card has a "Where the points came from" list for the current game.

    Loadouts are learned separately. Each fighter tracks how well every main gun, alt gun and utility has scored and picks better ones more often, while still trying the others now and then.

    Progress saves to this browser automatically. Export brains to move them to another device or to ship them with the site. The 8 highest-action games and the latest game are kept as exact replays under Best games.

    Keys: Space pauses, R toggles rays, 1 to 6 set speed, Esc opens this menu.
